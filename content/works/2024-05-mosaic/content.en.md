Mosaic is a photographic work exploring the tension between censorship and expression, based on the technique of photo mosaic.

The work began with an everyday question. Watching tattoos and smoking scenes blurred out on television, YouTube, films, and dramas, there were moments where it felt like too much. Censorship remains a heavily debated issue. In a liberal democratic society, where does ethical and moral censorship end and freedom of expression begin — that boundary is what this work set out to examine.

A figure holding a cigarette serves as the central image, a subject frequently targeted by censorship in contemporary media, chosen to emphasize its symbolic weight. The cigarette is left fully visible while the surrounding areas are rendered through photo mosaic. Within the mosaic, everyday photographs and images related to censorship are layered together using image processing techniques. Through the contrast of positive and negative imagery — and the inversion that mosaic itself implies — the work makes the conflict between censorship and expression visible.

The work did not fully realize the depth of thought the subject deserved, and there are parts that fell short of what was intended. Even so, the hope is that it offers a small opportunity to think about censorship — where it is necessary, and where it is not.
